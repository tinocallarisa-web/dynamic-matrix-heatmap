Let your report readers build their own crosstab — without edit rights, without touching the model,
and without the double-counting that breaks distinct counts in a pivot.

Dynamic Matrix Heatmap puts three dropdowns inside the visual: X dimension, Y dimension, and value.
Anyone reading the report picks the combination they need. Cells are coloured by a global heatmap
scale, so the pattern is visible before you read a single number.

The part that matters most is underneath. Power BI evaluates a measure at the granularity of every
field in the visual. Show only two of them and the rest have to be recombined — sums, minimums and
maximums recombine exactly, but a distinct count does not: a customer appearing under two categories
gets counted twice. This visual keeps the underlying rows and counts unique values itself, so the
figure is exact for every combination and the grand total matches your card visual.

PRIVACY

The visual makes no network requests of any kind. No analytics, no telemetry, no external scripts.
Its privileges array is empty, so Power BI blocks outbound connections at the platform level. The
only thing it saves is which fields you picked in the dropdowns, inside your own report file.

A NOTE ON ACCURACY

Sum, minimum, maximum and count re-aggregate exactly when projecting onto your chosen pair of
dimensions. Average is an unweighted approximation. A distinct count from a DAX measure cannot be
recombined correctly by any client — that is exactly what the "Distinct count of" field well is for.
All of this is documented rather than hidden.
